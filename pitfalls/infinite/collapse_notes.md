# Carry equivalence and infinite limits: exact obstruction and safe completions

These notes concern the finite signed Laurent-grid algebra

\[
A=K[X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}],\qquad C=X+Y-b,
\]

with finite carry equivalence modulo the ideal `(C)`. The coefficient field is
specified where needed. All proofs below are direct deductions from this model;
they are not claims of new results in the mathematical literature. The
coefficientwise counterexample also works over the integers. It does not assert
that each displayed grid is a bounded-digit implementation normal form.

## 1. The previously observed unweighted norm collapse

For real or rational coefficients and `b>2`, put

\[
P_n=b^{-n}(X+Y)^n.
\]

Every finite `P_n` represents the constant 1. Yet its raw coefficient `l1` norm
is `(2/b)^n`, which tends to zero. Therefore no continuous map from raw grids
with that norm to a nontrivial Hausdorff value space can both respect all finite
carries and distinguish 1 from 0.

Equivalently, in the unweighted absolutely summable convolution algebra,

\[
C^{-1}=-\frac1b\sum_{n\ge0}\left(\frac{X+Y}{b}\right)^n
\]

converges absolutely. Declaring `C=0` in an algebra in which `C` is already
invertible makes `1=C*C^{-1}=0`. This is an incompatible choice of completion,
not a contradiction in the finite algebra.

At the balanced evaluation `X=Y=b/2`, the appropriate absolute position weights
are `(b/2)^(i+j)`. Under those weights each `P_n` has norm exactly 1. Counting
the marks without counting the value of their positions caused the earlier
norm to miss what was conserved.

## 2. A stronger obstruction, including integer grids at every base

Define

\[
R_n=\left(\frac{b-Y}{X}\right)^n
 =\sum_{k=0}^n {n\choose k}b^{n-k}(-1)^kX^{-n}Y^k.
\]

Under the carry relation, `(b-Y)/X=1`, so every `R_n` represents 1. All its
coefficients lie in column `i=-n`. Consequently every fixed finite viewing
window is eventually empty: `R_n` tends coefficientwise to the zero grid.
Its coefficients are integers whenever `b` is an integer.

**No-go theorem.** Let `q` be a map on all finite signed integer Laurent grids
which respects finite carry equivalence and is continuous for coefficientwise
grid convergence into a Hausdorff space. Then `q` must assign the same value to
every finite grid as to the zero grid.

**Proof.** For any fixed finite grid `P`, the grids `P*R_n` are carry-equivalent
to `P`. Their support escapes to the left, so they tend coefficientwise to zero.
Thus the constant sequence `q(P*R_n)=q(P)` tends to `q(0)`. Uniqueness of limits
in a Hausdorff space gives `q(P)=q(0)`.

This result only concerns unrestricted raw-grid coefficientwise limits. It does
not rule out a separately proved canonical representation with a suitable
topology, nor directional Laurent-series spaces with restricted support.

With rational coefficients one can use the simpler positive grid `P_n` in
Section 1: its support has total degree `n`, so it too tends coefficientwise to
zero at every positive base, even when its `l1` norm does not tend to zero.

## 3. The two opposite fractional expansions cannot both be ordinary limits

Let `X,Y` be commuting invertible elements in a nontrivial Hausdorff topological
ring whose multiplication is jointly continuous. Consider the partial sums

\[
L_N=\sum_{n=0}^N(-1)^nX^{-n-1}Y^n,
\qquad
R_N=\sum_{n=0}^N(-1)^nY^{-n-1}X^n.
\]

**No-go theorem.** Both sequences cannot converge in this one topology.

**Proof.** Set `a=-Y/X`, which is invertible. If `L_N` converges, its successive
differences `X^{-1}a^N` tend to zero, hence `a^N` tends to zero. If `R_N`
converges, `a^{-N}` tends to zero. Joint continuity would imply
`1=a^N*a^{-N}` tends to `0*0=0`, contradicting uniqueness of limits.

This proof needs neither a norm nor the carry relation. In particular, a clever
new norm cannot make both of these opposite geometric series converge as
ordinary partial sums in one coherent topological arithmetic.

There is no obstacle to keeping both as directional expansions of the same
rational function, with different convergence domains, or applying a separately
defined summation prescription. Such a prescription must not be confused with
ordinary partial-sum convergence.

## 4. Exact survival criterion for simple weighted `l1` completions

Take complex coefficients and `r,s>0`. Complete finite Laurent grids in

\[
\|P\|_{r,s}=\sum_{i,j}|d_{ij}|r^is^j.
\]

The weights are multiplicative, so convolution is well defined and
submultiplicative; this is a unital commutative Banach algebra. Quotient by the
**closed** ideal generated by `C`.

For a real base `b>0`, the quotient is nonzero exactly when

\[
\boxed{|r-s|\le b\le r+s.}
\]

**Proof of survival.** These triangle inequalities say that complex numbers
`z,w` exist with `|z|=r`, `|w|=s`, and `z+w=b`. Evaluation at `(z,w)` is a bounded
algebra map, since `|P(z,w)| <= ||P||_(r,s)`. It annihilates `C` and therefore
its closed ideal, but sends 1 to 1. The quotient cannot be zero.

**Proof of collapse outside the triangle.** If `b>r+s`, the geometric inverse in
Section 1 converges in the weighted norm. If `r>b+s`, write

\[
C=X\left(1+\frac{Y-b}{X}\right),
\qquad \left\|\frac{Y-b}{X}\right\|_{r,s}=\frac{s+b}{r}<1,
\]

so again `C` has a geometric inverse. The case `s>b+r` is symmetric. These are
all ways the triangle inequalities can fail.

Thus the unweighted choice `r=s=1` survives for `0<b<=2` and collapses for
`b>2`. The numerical threshold depends on coordinate scaling; it is not evidence
of a physical transition. A surviving quotient is not automatically a faithful
model of every finite MDN function: which evaluations remain visible matters.

The expansion `L` belongs to this raw weighted algebra precisely when `s<r`,
with norm `1/(r-s)`; `R` belongs precisely when `r<s`. Neither belongs when
`r=s`. This is a concrete version of the theorem in Section 3.

## 5. A completion that preserves a whole interval of MDN values

Choose a closed interval `I=[a,c]` strictly inside `(0,b)`, with `a<c`. Interpret
a finite grid as the continuous function `P(x,b-x)` and use

\[
\|[P]\|_I=\sup_{x\in I}|P(x,b-x)|.
\]

This is a true norm on the finite real carry quotient: a rational function
vanishing on the whole interval is identically zero. It respects multiplication
and all finite carries. Every `P_n` and every `R_n` above has norm 1, so the
false collapse is impossible.

The completion of this real algebra is `C(I)`, the algebra of continuous real
functions on `I`, because ordinary polynomials already lie in it and are
uniformly dense. That is a mathematically coherent choice of infinite objects,
but not a unique compulsory choice. If holomorphic rather than continuous
limits are desired, one should use complex domains and locally uniform
convergence instead.

For an explicit sufficient condition on infinite grid coefficients, define

\[
w_{ij}=\sup_{x\in I}x^i(b-x)^j,
\qquad
\|d\|_w=\sum_{i,j}|d_{ij}|w_{ij}.
\]

These finite positive weights satisfy `w_(u+v)<=w_u*w_v`. Finite norm therefore
guarantees absolute uniform convergence on `I`, closure under convolution, and
safe rearrangement of the sums. Evaluation on every point of `I` is bounded by
this norm. The closed carry ideal stays proper because those evaluations send
1 to 1. One may quotient by the kernel of the interval-evaluation map to identify
all representations of the same function; no claim that this kernel always
equals the closed principal carry ideal is needed here.

On `I` lying wholly to the right of `b/2`, the left expansion has the rigorous
tail bound

\[
\left|L_N(x,b-x)-\frac1b\right|
 =\frac1b\left(\frac{b-x}{x}\right)^{N+1}
 \le \frac{q^{N+1}}b,
\quad q=\sup_{x\in I}\frac{b-x}{x}<1.
\]

The right expansion works on intervals to the left. At `x=b/2`, the ordinary
partial sums alternate between `0` and `2/b`; they do not converge. Averaging
or damping can assign `1/b`, but those are explicitly additional definitions.

## Plain-language conclusion

The forbidden step is treating “every fixed cell eventually looks empty” as
“the represented quantity has become zero.” Carries can transport the complete
value outside every fixed window. Defining limits by the actual functions, or
by coefficient weights that control those functions, avoids that mistake.
Different directional expansions can be useful descriptions of the same object
without being simultaneously convergent digit strings. Infinity requires a
specified viewpoint and error rule; it does not provide an exception to exact
finite identities.

## Context in established mathematics

- Karlheinz Gröchenig, [Weight Functions in Time-Frequency
  Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611174), surveys the role of
  submultiplicative weights in convolution algebras. The concrete MDN triangle
  criterion above is proved here directly by evaluation and geometric series.
- Darij Grinberg's scribe notes for Victor Kac, [Fundamentals of formal
  distributions](https://darijgrinberg.gitlab.io/algebra/algebra.html), develop
  Laurent series and formal distributions. Formal distributions allow broader
  support than ordinary Laurent series, but arbitrary products need not exist.
